# Steak with Bordelaise Sauce

Serves 2.

A pan-seared sirloin finished under a Bordeaux, shallot, and bone marrow reduction. The overlooked ingredient is bone marrow — added off the heat at the end, it gives the sauce a gloss and body no amount of cream can match. Classic bistro steak frites when served with fries.

## Ingredients

### Steak

- 400g sirloin steak, at room temperature for 2 hours (about 14 oz — ribeye or flat iron also work)
- Salt and pepper
- Tallow, or another high-smoke-point cooking fat

### Bordelaise bone marrow sauce

- 2 banana shallots, finely sliced
- 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, plus more to finish
- 2 bone marrow canoes, scraped raw and chopped
- 70ml red wine vinegar (about 1/4 cup)
- 250ml Bordeaux, or another dry red wine (about 1 cup)
- 250ml good beef or veal stock (about 1 cup)
- 10g flat-leaf parsley, chopped (about 2 tablespoons)
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper

### To serve (optional)

- French fries (frites)

## Instructions

### Cook and rest the steak

- Heat the oven to 70°C / 160°F
- Salt the steaks just before they hit the pan
- Sear over medium heat in tallow for 2 to 3 minutes, turning every 20 to 30 seconds
- Transfer to a rack and rest in the oven for 10 minutes
- Return to the pan for 30 seconds to build the crust, then back into the oven — drop the oven to 50°C / 120°F while you make the sauce

### Build the sauce

- Keep the pan on low heat with all the fond still in it
- Add the sliced shallots, a little of the chopped bone marrow, some of the butter, and a pinch of salt and pepper
- Cook for 5 to 7 minutes, until the shallots are translucent

### Deglaze and reduce

- Pour in the red wine vinegar and cook until the shallots are jammy
- Add the Bordeaux, turn the heat up high, and reduce for 8 to 10 minutes — until a spoon leaves a clear trace across the pan
- Add the stock and reduce for another 5 to 7 minutes, until it coats the back of a spoon
- Turn the heat to low

### Finish

- Add the remaining bone marrow and the rest of the butter
- Swirl the pan constantly for about a minute until the sauce emulsifies into a glossy finish
- Off the heat, stir in the chopped parsley
- Taste and adjust salt

### Plate

- Take the steak out of the oven
- Spoon sauce onto the plate, steak on top, dress with the resting juices
- Serve with frites and a little extra sauce spooned over the steak

## Tips

- Bone marrow canoes are butcher-cut lengthwise — scrape the raw marrow out with a small spoon and chop before you start
- The vinegar amount looks aggressive on paper, but the acid cooks off and the fatty marrow balances what remains
- Do not skimp on the reduction times; the sauce depends on the wine and stock being fully concentrated before the marrow goes in

## Substitutions

- Bone marrow: an extra 2 tablespoons of cold butter mounted in at the finish gets you close on mouthfeel, but not on depth
- Bordeaux: any dry red — Cabernet, Merlot, or Côtes du Rhône
- Banana shallots: 3-4 small round shallots
- Tallow: clarified butter, ghee, or a neutral high-smoke-point oil
