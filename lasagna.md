# Lasagna

Frank Proto's restaurant-quality lasagna (Epicurious 101). The whole idea is building layered flavor: brown each thing off in the same pot, season as you go, don't just dump everything in at once.

## Ingredients

### Meat sauce

- Olive oil
- Pork country-style ribs — good fat-to-meat ratio, gets shredded back in
- Spicy Italian sausage, casings removed
- 1 large onion, diced
- 2 small carrots, diced
- 2 ribs celery, diced
- 4–6 cloves garlic, minced
- Dry white wine (a generous splash to deglaze)
- 2–3 cans (28 oz) whole peeled tomatoes, hand-crushed or run through a food mill
- Fresh basil (a small bunch — half tied with butcher's twine as a bundle, half reserved and shredded)
- Chili flakes
- Salt and black pepper

### Cheese mix and layers

- Whole-milk ricotta
- Low-moisture mozzarella, shredded
- Pecorino Romano, grated
- Parmigiano-Reggiano, grated
- Dry lasagna noodles (no need to boil — the sauce hydrates them)

## Instructions

### Make the sauce (about 2 hours, mostly hands-off)

1. Heat a heavy pot over medium-high with a good glug of olive oil. Season the pork ribs with salt and pepper and brown them on 2–3 sides, 5–7 minutes total. Don't crowd the pot. Pull them out with a slotted spoon and set aside.
2. Add the sausage in crumbles to the same pot. Break it up and brown well. Remove to the bowl with the pork. Leave the fat and fond in the pot — that's your flavor base.
3. Drop the heat to medium. Add the onions with a pinch of salt. Cook until you hear "snap, crackle, pop" and the edges just start to color.
4. Add the carrots and celery. Cook until softened, 4–5 minutes.
5. Add the garlic and cook until fragrant, about 30 seconds — don't let it burn.
6. Deglaze with the white wine, scraping the bottom. Let it reduce until the raw-alcohol smell is gone.
7. Tie half the basil into a bundle with butcher's twine and drop it in.
8. Add the tomatoes. Swirl a little water in each empty can and add that too. Season with salt, pepper, and chili flakes.
9. Return the pork and sausage (plus any juices) to the pot. Bring to a simmer, then drop to medium-low and cook uncovered for about 1½ hours, stirring occasionally. Add splashes of water if it tightens up too much.
10. Fish out the basil bundle and discard. Pull the pork out, shred it with two forks, and stir it back into the sauce. Stir in the reserved shredded basil. Taste and adjust salt.

### Assemble

1. Preheat oven to 350°F.
2. Mix the mozzarella, Pecorino, and Parmigiano together in a bowl. Keep the ricotta separate — you'll dab it on, not spread it.
3. Ladle a thin layer of sauce on the bottom of a baking dish.
4. Layer in this order, repeating until you're near the top:
   - Dry lasagna noodles (overlap edges slightly)
   - Sauce
   - A handful of the mixed cheeses
   - Small dabs of ricotta scattered across (don't try to cover — you want pockets)
5. Top layer: noodles, sauce, and only the mixed cheeses — no ricotta on top (it dries out and cracks).

### Bake

1. Set the dish on a sheet tray to catch drips. Cover loosely with foil.
2. Bake covered at 350°F for 30 minutes.
3. Remove the foil and bake another 30 minutes, until the top is browned and bubbly.
4. **Rest at least 30 minutes before cutting.** This is not optional — it's what lets the layers set so slices hold their shape.

## Notes

- No-boil noodles work; regular dry noodles also work as long as the sauce is loose enough to hydrate them. If your sauce is thick, thin with a little water before assembling.
- Bigger batch of sauce than you need? Freeze the extra — it's better the next day.
